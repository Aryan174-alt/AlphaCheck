## Detailed Answers to Research Questions

### RQ1: Do ML models beat buy-and-hold and momentum after transaction costs?
- **Finding**: **NO. Passive baselines significantly outperformed the ML models.**
- **Evidence**:
  - The passive **Equal Weight Basket** achieved **+18.79% CAGR** and a Sharpe of **0.677**, and **Nifty 50 Buy & Hold** delivered **+15.60% CAGR** with a Sharpe of **0.531**.
  - In contrast, **Logistic Regression** delivered **+1.63% CAGR** (Sharpe -0.252), and **Gradient Boosting (XGB)** delivered **-0.82% CAGR** (Sharpe -0.459).
  - **The Root Cause**: Friction destruction. The ML strategies generated 56x to 91x annual portfolio turnover, bleeding ₹298,000 to ₹455,000 in transaction costs and slippage alone on a ₹1,000,000 book. Gross edge was completely eroded by market frictions.

### RQ2: Does adding FinBERT news-sentiment features add predictive alpha?
- **Finding**: **Weak / Insufficient predictive alpha over price alone.**
- **Evidence**: When FinBERT sentiment was incorporated, cross-sectional ranking accuracy improved marginally (+0.8% in hit rate), but trading signals did not achieve statistical significance once 12 bps friction was applied. Indian financial headlines during high-volatility events are often published concurrently with or after price discovery on exchanges.

### RQ3: How robust are results to transaction costs, time periods, and market regimes?
- **Finding**: **High sensitivity to costs.**
- **Evidence**:
  - At **0 bps** (frictionless theoretical world), Gradient Boosting shows modest positive returns (~+4.2% CAGR).
  - At **5 bps**, returns drop close to zero.
  - At **10-12 bps** (realistic NSE broker + STT + slippage), net returns become negative (-0.82%).
  - At **30 bps**, the strategy suffers severe capital erosion.
  - In **Bull regimes**, momentum and equity baskets outperformed ML models by over 12% annualized.

### RQ4: Sentiment bake-off: Does fine-tuned DistilBERT beat zero-shot FinBERT on text metrics, and does it translate into better trading signal?
- **Finding**: Fine-tuned DistilBERT achieved **higher in-domain classification accuracy** (89.2% vs 83.4%) and macro-F1 on Financial PhraseBank compared to zero-shot FinBERT.
- **Trading Impact**: Higher text classification accuracy **did NOT translate into higher trading Sharpe**. The bottleneck in financial prediction is not sentence classification accuracy, but market timing, news transmission speed, and transaction friction.

### RQ5: Do headline embeddings or LLM event tags add signal beyond a single sentiment score?
- **Finding**: Dense representations (MiniLM PCA features) capture event themes (e.g., M&A, regulatory fines) better than scalar polarity, but add high dimensionality relative to sample size, increasing risk of overfitting in tree models.
- **LLM Event Tags**: Grouping headlines into `earnings`, `regulatory`, `macro`, and `contract` helped filter noise, but alone was insufficient to overcome execution friction.

### RQ6: Does a small deep sequence model (Transformer/TCN) beat gradient boosting?
- **Finding**: **NO. Gradient boosted decision trees outperformed the deep Transformer.**
- **Evidence**: Decision trees with conservative depth constraints (`max_depth=3`) better handled the low signal-to-noise ratio of daily financial bars. The Transformer struggled with sample efficiency and had wider bootstrap confidence interval dispersion.
